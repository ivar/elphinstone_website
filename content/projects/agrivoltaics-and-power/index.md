{{< term "Agrivoltaics" >}}Using the same piece of land for both farming and solar electricity, with panels mounted high or spaced widely enough that crops or animals can share the ground beneath them.{{< /term >}} treats sunlight as something a field can harvest twice: once by the plants, and once by the panels above or beside them.

## Sharing the light

Most crops can't use all the sun they get on a bright summer day. Past a certain point, extra light only adds heat and water stress. Panels that cast moving, partial shade can take that surplus and turn it into electricity, while the plants underneath lose less water and stay cooler. Shade-tolerant crops such as leafy greens and berries tend to cope best; sun-hungry crops give up more yield.

## Power on a farm

A farm's electricity has plenty of work to do: pumping water, running fans and heat in hoop houses, keeping produce cold, charging tools. Sizing a system for that starts with the same basics as any household: knowing your loads, what your wiring can carry, and how solar, batteries and the grid fit together. That is what our Home Power Basics course covers.
