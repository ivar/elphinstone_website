Native plants evolved alongside the pollinators, birds, fungi and soil life of the {{< term "Salish Sea bioregion" >}}The coastal and island ecosystem of southwestern BC and northwestern Washington, defined by its shared watershed and ecology rather than political borders.{{< /term >}}. Because of those long relationships, they tend to support far more of the local food web than imported ornamentals, and once established they generally need less water and care.

## What we're growing

**Oregon grape** is an evergreen shrub with holly-like leaves, yellow flowers in early spring — an important early food source for pollinators — and tart blue berries. We're selecting and propagating the best-tasting plants we can find.

**Salal** is a tough evergreen understory shrub with dark berries that can be eaten fresh or dried. It thrives in the shade of coastal forests.

**Red elderberry** grows quickly and feeds many birds. Its raw berries, seeds, leaves and stems can make people ill, so it's valued more for habitat than for the kitchen.

**Camas** is a blue-flowering bulb that was a staple food for Coast Salish peoples, tended in carefully managed meadows. It has a toxic look-alike, death camas, so it should only be harvested by people who know the difference.

**Riparian species** grow along streams and wetlands, where their roots hold banks together and their shade keeps water cool for fish and other aquatic life.
