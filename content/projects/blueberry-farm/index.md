We're struggling right now with a major "mummy berry" infestation. 

{{< details "Mummy Berry" >}}
from the paper (*1)
"Mummy berry is a blueberry disease caused by the fungus, Monilinia vaccinii-corymbosi. In early spring, infected fruit laying on the ground will produce a small mushroom and disperse spores, which initiate infection. It is commonly recommended that growers manage mummy berry by cultivating the soil, removing infected fruit, or spraying fungicides. However, many Pacific Northwest growers still have trouble managing mummy berry, especially in organic production. In the present study, mulching with 5 cm (2 inches) of Douglas-fir sawdust suppressed mushroom development in comparison with bare ground. Douglas-fir sawdust at a 2.5 cm (1 inch) depth varied in effectiveness, while 2.5 cm of blueberry leaves had no effect on mushroom development. Mulch application timing did not affect subsequent mushroom development. However, mulches lose more depth when applied at the beginning of the autumn season as compared with late winter, which may affect mushroom development in the spring. Therefore, an annual application of at least 5 cm (2 inches) of Douglas-fir sawdust either in late winter (prior to blueberry bud break) or anytime during the fall and winter months if maintained at or above the 5 cm minimum depth is recommended to help manage mummy berry for Pacific Northwest growers."
